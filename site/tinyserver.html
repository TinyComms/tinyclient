Tiny server

Your own voice server, with tinyclient as its app.

Voice channels in categories, laid out the way Discord lays out a server, with calls that are end-to-end encrypted. One server holds one community, and whoever runs it decides who joins.

Run your own Download tinyclient

Self-hosted · Open source, MIT · One community per server · Voice, no text chat

The server opens on its home: its name, description and welcome, who is in which voice channel, and who is online.

  • Voice channels in categories that fold, each showing how many are in it and how many fit.
  • People seated under their channel, with a ring while they speak and a mark when they are muted or deafened.
  • Double-click a channel to join. Drag someone to another channel to move them, or drag channels to reorder.
  • The member list groups people by role, with presence and where each one sits.

In tinyclient

A server you already know how to use

A Tiny server sits on tinyclient’s rail beside your other networks, and everything about it happens in the same window.

Voice that sounds right

A channel shows a tile for each person in the call. The bar at the bottom mutes, deafens, picks devices and shows how the connection is doing.

  • Echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain, on by default.
  • Voice activation with a threshold you can see, or push to talk. A channel can require push to talk.
  • Keys that work while tinyclient is in the background, on Windows, macOS and X11.
  • Your own volume for each person, 0 to 200 %, or mute them just for you.
  • Call health in plain words: ping, jitter, loss and the path the call takes. A lost connection rejoins by itself.
  • Calls go over UDP, or over TCP on the same port where a network blocks UDP. Up to 12 people in a channel.

End-to-end encrypted calls

When every app in a call can, the call is end-to-end encrypted. The apps form an MLS group, the design Discord uses for its own calls, and the server forwards audio it cannot unlock.

  • New keys whenever someone joins or leaves, so nobody hears what was said before they came or after they went.
  • A 30-digit call code everyone in the call sees. The same code means one encrypted group, not two halves joined by the server.
  • A safety number for each person, to compare and mark as verified. tinyclient warns when a verified person’s key changes.
  • A channel can require encryption. Where an app can’t encrypt, the call says “Encrypted to the server” and names who.

Moderation where the person is

Right-click anyone, on a seat, a tile, the member list or the home, for the same menu. It holds only what you may do to them.

  • Server mute and server deafen, move to another channel, or disconnect from the call.
  • Time out for a minute up to a week. Kick or ban with a reason; a ban can end after a day, a week, a month, or never.
  • Give and take roles. You act only on people below your own highest role.
  • A ban covers every way that person signs in, and signs them out everywhere at once.

Server Settings

Run the whole server from tinyclient

The server has no admin website. Its owners, and the roles they trust, manage it in tinyclient, and each page shows only what that person may change.

Roles and permissions

Make roles, drag them into order, give each a colour, and switch on what it may do. Twenty permissions cover joining and speaking, video, priority speaker, invites, channels, roles, kicks, bans, timeouts, moving people and the audit log.

  • A higher role outranks the ones below it.
  • Nobody can hand out a permission they don’t hold.
  • Owners sit above every role, and the last owner can’t be removed.

Channels

Create channels and categories and drag them into place. Each channel has a topic, a limit on how many can join, and a bitrate from 16 to 128 kb/s.

  • Push to talk required, per channel.
  • Per-role overrides: allow, deny or inherit each permission in this channel alone.

Members

Everyone on the server, with their roles, when they joined and when they were last seen. Search by name or email, filter by role, and tick several people to change their roles or remove them together.

  • Email shows only to those who manage people.
  • People who came in through a sign-in group are marked.

Invites

An invite is one code. It holds the server’s address, so the person you send it to pastes it into tinyclient and never types an address.

  • Choose the roles it gives, a label, how many times it works, and when it expires: 7 days and one use unless you say otherwise.
  • The code is shown once. Revoke any invite still open, or all of them.

Audit log

What was done on the server, by whom, and why, in plain sentences. Filter by person and by kind of change, and read back as far as you need.

  • Roles, members, invites, bans, timeouts, channels and voice moderation are all written down.
  • A kick, ban or timeout keeps its reason.

Overview

The name, icon, description and welcome message everyone sees on the home, and where people land: the default channel, and the AFK channel with its timeout.

Voice & media

Whether the server’s audio is up, the address and port calls use, and each channel’s limit, bitrate and talk mode, on one page. If people get a seat and hear nothing, it says which port to open.

Joining

Paste a code, sign in in your browser

No password to make. tinyclient opens your browser at the server’s sign-in provider, Pocket ID with a passkey unless the owner chose another, and the browser hands tinyclient back a one-time code. tinyclient never sees a password.

  • One codeThe invite holds the server, so nobody types an address.
  • PasskeysNew people make their account from the invite, with a passkey.
  • GroupsAn owner can let a provider’s groups in, or make them admins. Never owners.
  • Several serversJoin as many as you like; each keeps its own sign-in.

Your account

Your name, and every session

Settings › Accounts lists each server you joined. Change the name people there see, or sign out on every computer at once. A sign-in lasts 30 days unless the owner sets another length.

Sign-in, in Server Settings

Which provider the server signs in through, how many people came in through its groups, and who you are signed in as, with your user ID and Sign out everywhere.

Hosting it

One Compose file, one open port

tinyserver is written in Rust and runs with Docker Compose on a machine you own. The repository builds the image; there is no hosted version.

What runs

  • tinyserverThe API, the live connection each app keeps open, and the voice relay. A read-only container with no added privileges.
  • Postgres 17The server’s data. Permissions are checked in the database itself.
  • Pocket IDSign-in with passkeys. Any OpenID Connect provider works instead: Authentik, Keycloak, Zitadel, Google.
  • Cloudflare TunnelCarries the web traffic for two hostnames, one for the server and one for sign-in. No web port opens on the host.
  • Port 50000UDP and TCP, forwarded on the router, for call audio. The only port published.

Start it

# in deploy/, with the environment file filled in
sh pocket-id/setup.sh your-username
docker compose up -d
docker compose logs tinyserver | grep -A2 'Owner invite'

The first start prints an owner invite, good once and for 24 hours. Paste it into tinyclient’s Join a server: whoever signs in with it owns the server. Lost it? tinyserver bootstrap --owner-invite makes a new one.

For calls, set TINYSERVER_PUBLIC_IP to your public address and forward port 50000, UDP and TCP. GET /health should then say "media": "up" with "transports": ["udp", "tcp"]. Without a public address the server runs, but calls have no sound.

What the server can see

Encrypted calls, and what is still visible

End-to-end encryption keeps what is said from the server. It does not hide that a call happened. tinyclient’s call panel says the same.

The server cannot

  • Hear an encrypted call. It forwards audio it has no key for.
  • Join a call unseen. Anyone added appears in the call’s list, with a safety number.
  • Swap a verified person’s key without tinyclient warning that it changed.
  • Read a password. It keeps none; signing in is the provider’s job.

The server can see

  • Who is in which call, and when.
  • Who is speaking and how loudly, for the speaking ring.
  • Roughly how much audio each person sends.
  • Each member’s network address. Members see the server’s.
  • Who is on the server, their roles, and the audit log.

Whoever runs the server could also add someone nobody has verified, or turn a call to encrypted-to-the-server by claiming an app can’t encrypt. tinyclient shows both. The threat model →

  • Text chatA Tiny server has voice channels and no messages. Chat with the same people on the networks tinyclient already has.
