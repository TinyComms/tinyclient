# 06 — Calls

What tinyclient carries in a call, and what it still hands to the official app. Code on main is the source. A call button shows an in-call state only after the network confirms the join. A network or call type this file does not list as native is a hand-off: the control names the official app and does not start mediad.

## Sound

Discord calls and Tiny room calls use the same microphone processing. Echo cancellation, noise suppression, and automatic gain are on unless Settings › Voice turns one off. Noise suppression defaults to Enhanced, which turns down other voices and cries as well as fans. Standard and High leave other voices in. The canceller hears the mix that is playing, cues included. The Tiny channel bar says to wear headphones only while echo cancellation is off.

Discord voice is Opus at 48 kHz stereo: voice gateway v8, UDP, and DAVE. DAVE seals Opus, and on a picture it seals the H.264 access unit. Version 1 is required on every Discord call this client joins. A stage is not joined; it opens in Discord. A voice channel joins from the server list: the hover Join pill, a double-click, or Join voice on the row menu. A single click opens the channel and does not join. The Discord home lists friends who are in a voice channel or a DM call under In voice, and that row joins the same way. A DM can be called and can ring. A camera rides that same voice connection. A screen is a separate Go Live connection, described under Pictures on a Discord call.

A Tiny room joins with sound when the server's audio is up: one send-only Opus microphone (mono), one receive slot per other seat, UDP preferred, and ICE-TCP where UDP is blocked. Mute, deafen, speaking, and join and leave cues are part of that call. If the server has no audio, joining only takes a seat.

## Direct calls on a Tiny server

When a Tiny server advertises `direct_calls`, members can place 1:1 audio and video calls from a direct message conversation header or from a person's menu (bots cannot be called).

- **Ringing:** outgoing calls ring until answered, declined, cancelled, or timed out (60 s); incoming calls show in the call dock and as a system notification with Accept and Decline. Accepting starts `mediad` and joins the 1:1 media session; declining or cancelling ends it and records a call row in the conversation.
- **Media and auto-watch:** direct calls carry the same microphone processing, camera, screen share, and optional MLS `e2ee_calls` sealing as Tiny rooms. As soon as the peer turns on their camera or screen share, tinyclient automatically begins decoding and displaying it above the conversation (with a corner self-view PiP when you send your own camera or screen, and a compact preview tile above the dock if you navigate to another chat). Choosing **Stop watching** stops decoding until the peer stops and starts again.
- **Ending:** either side leaving ends the call for them; when the peer hangs up, the dock briefly shows "Call ended" before clearing.

## Pictures, on a Tiny room or direct call

A Tiny call can send a camera and a screen, and it receives H.264 video. Desktop calls try the machine's hardware first. Windows uses Media Foundation to encode and its D3D11-backed H.264 decoder to receive. macOS uses hardware-required VideoToolbox compression and decompression sessions. Linux tries FFmpeg's VA-API or NVENC encoder and its VA-API or NVDEC decoder. Linux loads supported system FFmpeg libraries and GPU drivers at runtime when present.

Calls send constrained-baseline H.264 without frame reordering. If hardware is unavailable or stops working, the track switches to OpenH264. The sender begins the software stream with a fresh keyframe. The receiver resumes at a keyframe, requesting one if needed. On desktop, Auto camera can use the 720p, 30-fps profile when the hardware probe succeeds. The Tiny call's Video stats list the backends that actually produced frames during the last sample, including separate entries when camera and screen sharing use different encoders.

Mediad prepares the OpenH264 fallback in the background. The binary is downloaded from Cisco, checked, and cached in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\tinyclient` on Windows or `~/.cache/tinyclient` elsewhere. The download is tried three times with a growing pause. If it fails, the call stays voice and the dock explains how to retry by turning the share or camera on again.

The room or direct call forwards one picture: a share while someone else is sharing, otherwise one layer of another person's camera. The listener does not get their own picture back from the server; local self-view renders directly from the outgoing capture. The client offers three video lines, the first two inactive until asked: a camera with adaptive layers, one screen share at the quality chosen in the share picker (`ShareQuality`: **Best** up to 2560×1440 at up to 60 fps, **Balanced** 1080p at 30 fps, or **Low** 720p at 15 fps), and one receive line for the picture forwarded by the server. In a Tiny room, nothing decodes until someone chooses Watch; in a 1:1 Tiny direct call, the peer's camera or screen share is auto-watched as soon as it starts.

The camera is the system's camera: Media Foundation on Windows, AVFoundation on macOS, and V4L2 on Linux. A Linux camera that V4L2 cannot open is asked for through the desktop portal, the same request on GNOME, KDE, Sway, Hyprland, and the other desktops that ship one. Android and iOS use the phone's own camera. A machine with no camera says so and the call stays voice.

On Windows, macOS and X11 Linux, the share picker lists individual displays and windows with thumbnails. Pick a window to share it as it resizes. Closing it ends the share. On macOS, allow tinyclient in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Screen & System Audio Recording, then restart tinyclient. macOS display shares exclude tinyclient’s own windows. X11 with a compositor can capture a covered window. Without a compositor, the share shows the window’s rectangle on screen, including anything covering it. A Wayland session asks the desktop portal, so the system's picker chooses a monitor or a window on every compositor that implements that portal. PipeWire is loaded when that share starts. A machine without it still launches, and says so if a Wayland share is asked for. Cancelling the picker does not fall through to a black picture. Android shares through MediaProjection. An iPhone shares the whole screen through a broadcast extension. The simulator, which has no system broadcast, shares this app's screen.

When the server offers `e2ee_calls`, the same MLS group seals Opus and the H.264 picture. Silence is not sealed. The picture's one-byte NAL header stays clear so the server can still see a keyframe. It cannot open the picture or the voice. Until the group has keys, the microphone and the picture are held back rather than sent in the clear. A server that does not offer `e2ee_calls` carries the call as transport encryption only. The lock and the call code in the call header are this protection: a Tiny server's MLS group, or Discord's DAVE. On Discord the lock opens the voice privacy code, the same for everyone in the call and new when someone joins or leaves, and a verification code for each person. A Go Live stream has its own code.

## Pictures, on a Discord call

A camera sends up to two H.264 simulcast layers on the voice connection: a primary high layer (`rid` `100`, up to 1280×720 at 30 fps when hardware encoding or HD quality is active, or 640×360 at 15 fps on standard software encoding) paired with a low simulcast layer (`rid` `50`, 640×360 at 15 fps beneath 720p or 320×180 at 15 fps beneath 360p) when Discord assigns a second SSRC pair. Go Live is a second voice connection. It sends the screen at the quality that was picked: 720p at 15 frames a second, 1080p at 30, or 1440p at 60. It does not open a microphone. Each watched Go Live stream opens its own receive-only leg with its own DAVE session, so multiple Go Live streams can be watched at the same time while every active camera on the voice connection continues decoding side by side in the call stage grid (within the device's concurrent decode budget; streams over budget report `WatchRefused`). Clicking any camera or stream tile focuses it and sends per-SSRC media sink wants (`op 15`) scaled by tile size (`100` for focused/large tiles, `50` for grid tiles). The stream's MLS group is the stream server id minus one, not the channel id.

When you watch one or more Go Live streams with sound, each stream's Opus audio mixes into the call's selected output with its own volume and mute controls. Deafening silences watched streams, and muting your microphone leaves them playing. DAVE on each watch connection opens that stream's sound and picture.

DAVE seals every video frame as well as the voice, following each codec's unencrypted-header rules (H.264 NAL headers and slice PPS bytes, VP8 10-byte keyframe / 1-byte delta headers, and AV1 OBU headers with the final OBU's size bit cleared). The transport encryption is the same AES-GCM or XChaCha the voice already uses. In Select Protocol, the client offers H.264 for encode and decode (priority 1000, keeping H.264 as the sole encode codec), VP8 for decode (RFC 7741 depacketizer + pure-Rust `vp8-decoder`, dual-licensed `(MIT OR Apache-2.0) AND BSD-3-Clause`, zero C/CMake dependencies), and AV1 for decode (AV1 RTP depacketizer + pure-Rust `rusty_av1d` built without assembly, `BSD-2-Clause`, zero nasm/C dependencies so cross-builds stay self-contained across Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, and iOS). There is no VP9 decode and no clips. A desktop share can include the sound of that screen or window: stereo Opus on the Go Live connection, with the call's own playback left out. A preview of that share is a small picture sent to Discord outside the encrypted stream, and the people watching it are the ones Discord names, including when the stream is paused. A stage is still not joined.

## Telegram 1:1 calls, screen share, group video chats, and encrypted conferences

Telegram 1:1 voice and video calls, 1:1 screen sharing with sound, group/channel voice chats, and encrypted group conferences run natively inside tinyclient using a pure-Rust media engine (`str0m` + `unsafe-libopus` + `sonora` + hardware H.264 / OpenH264 + MTProto 2.0 AES-256-IGE + pure-Rust `tde2e`-compatible conference E2EE) rather than linking C++ `tgcalls` or C++ `tde2e`, avoiding a C++/CMake/libwebrtc build dependency while sharing the same microphone processing, echo cancellation, camera/screen pickers, and audio loopback capture as Discord and Tiny calls.

- **Incoming calls:** `UpdatePhoneCall` (`phoneCallRequested`) acknowledges delivery (`phone.receivedCall`) and rings with caller name, audio/video indication (`Call::incoming_video`), system notification, and Accept / Decline (`phone.discardCall`). Answering or discarding on another device stops the ring cleanly.
- **1:1 voice and video calls (`#458`):** Full MTProto 2048-bit Diffie-Hellman key exchange (`messages.getDhConfig`, `phone.getCallConfig`, `phone.requestCall` with `video: true` for video calls, `phone.acceptCall`, `phone.confirmCall`) with safe-prime and range checks, `g_a_hash` commitment check, 64-bit `key_fingerprint` verification, and the 4 verification emoji shown in the call header. In-call frames and `phone.sendSignalingData` / `updatePhoneCallSignalingData` messages are sealed with MTProto 2.0 AES-256-IGE. Camera H.264 video negotiates over the in-call signaling channel alongside remote camera status (`active`, `inactive`, `paused`, and `low_battery`), reuses the desktop camera picker and mobile camera flip control, and preserves `video: true` on `phone.discardCall`. A second incoming call while already in a call is automatically declined as busy.
- **1:1 screen share with sound (`#448`):** Negotiates a second H.264 video track (`screencast`) beside the camera track in 1:1 in-call signaling. Uses the shared `share_audio` loopback capture (WASAPI on Windows, ScreenCaptureKit on macOS, PipeWire monitor on Linux, with playback echo subtraction) to mix system/window sound into the sent Telegram audio stream when Share Sound is enabled. Displays remote screencast alongside remote camera in multi-picture `Call::pictures` and shows `"is sharing their screen"` (`Participant::sharing`) in the call roster.
- **Encrypted group conferences (`#450`):**
  - `messageActionConferenceCall` maps to a structured call row (invited/live, missed, ended, with participants), and incoming conference invites ring with Accept and Decline (`phone.declineConferenceCallInvite`).
  - `t.me/call/<slug>` links resolve via `phone.getGroupCall(inputGroupCallSlug)` to display who is in the conference and offer **Join**.
  - Creating a shareable conference link uses `phone.createConferenceCall`; adding people to a running 1:1 call upgrades it cleanly into an encrypted conference via `phone.createConferenceCall` + `phone.inviteConferenceCallParticipant` + `phone.discardCall(MigrateConferenceCall)`; removing participants uses `phone.deleteConferenceCallParticipants`.
  - Chain blocks (`phone.getGroupCallChainBlocks`, `phone.sendConferenceCallBroadcast`, `updateGroupCallChainBlocks`) are verified before joining and on every update, deriving per-epoch AES-256-GCM frame keys and 4 verification emoji in the call header that update on every membership change. **Fail-closed invariant:** if any chain block fails verification, tinyclient refuses to join or terminates media (`SecurityMode::Blocked`, `required: true`) and never falls back to unencrypted media.

### Pure-Rust `tde2e`-compatible conference E2EE vs linking C++ `tde2e` from TDLib

| Dimension | Linking C++ `tde2e` from TDLib | Pure-Rust `tde2e`-compatible layer (`mediad::telegram_e2ee`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build toolchain | Requires CMake, C++20 compiler, OpenSSL/BoringSSL, and TDLib's build graph across 5 platforms (Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS). | Zero C/C++ build dependencies; builds with standard Cargo using existing `aes-gcm` and `sha2` crates already linked by `mediad`. |
| Binary size & auditability | Adds several MB of native C++ object code plus FFI glue and manual memory lifetime bridging across threads. | ~450 lines of memory-safe Rust directly integrated into `mediad`'s 20 ms media loop and `chatd`'s join gate. |
| Chain verification & fail-closed behavior | Full upstream TDLib blockchain state machine, opaque across FFI boundary. | Verifies sub-chain 0 (membership/state hash chain, monotonic height, `prev_hash`, authorized signer, canonical participant set) and sub-chain 1 (epoch broadcast), updates 4 verification emoji on every membership change, seals per-frame audio/video with AES-256-GCM, and fails closed (`SecurityMode::Blocked`, `required: true`) on any invalid block. |
| Trade-off | Automatic wire-level lockstep with future official Telegram `tde2e` schema revisions. | Must track upstream Telegram `tde2e` binary block TL revisions as Telegram evolves the conference E2EE spec. |

- **Live and scheduled video chats:** Opening a group or channel with an active video chat reads `chatFull.call` / `channelFull.call` and `phone.getGroupCall` once (never polling unopened chats), shows a live banner with title, count, and participants plus a `LIVE` badge in the sidebar, and clears on `groupCallDiscarded`. Scheduled video chats show a local-time service message, countdown banner, **Notify me** toggle (`phone.toggleGroupCallStartSubscription`), and admin controls to schedule (`phone.createGroupCall`) or **Start now** (`phone.startScheduledGroupCall`).
- **Joining group voice chats:** Joining sends local `str0m` WebRTC parameters (`phone.joinGroupCall`, muted by default), applies `updateGroupCallConnection` transport JSON, pages participants (`phone.getGroupParticipants`), runs `phone.checkGroupCall` keepalives, updates mute via `phone.editGroupCallParticipant`, reports speech via `speakingInGroupCallAction`, caps simultaneous decoded streams to the 12 loudest speakers, and leaves via `phone.leaveGroupCall`. Unencrypted group voice chats do not display the E2EE lock or emoji.
- **Group video chat controls:** Before joining, tinyclient queries `phone.getGroupCallJoinAs` and `groupcall_default_join_as` (skipping the picker when only the personal account is eligible, prompting when unreadable, saving per-chat defaults via `phone.saveDefaultGroupCallJoinAs`, rendering channel participants safely, and supporting mid-call identity switches). Users can invite explicitly selected people (`phone.inviteToGroupCall`) with per-person refusals, copy listener or admin speaker links (`phone.exportGroupCallInvite`), reset hashes (`phone.toggleGroupCallSettings`), and open `t.me/<name>?videochat[=hash]`, `?voicechat`, or `?livestream` links directly with Join. Admins with `manage_call` can start a video chat from a confirmation dialog (with optional title, RTMP option, and basic-group-to-supergroup migration), rename it (`phone.editGroupCallTitle`), toggle **New participants are muted** (`join_muted`), mute for everyone/allow-to-speak/remove-and-ban participants, and choose **End for everyone** (`phone.discardGroupCall`) when leaving. Suppressed listeners see a **Raise hand** control in place of the mic while `mediad` sends no audio, switching immediately back to a mic button once allowed to speak; the roster orders speakers first, then raised hands, then listeners, and displays `participants_count`.
- **Recording indicator and admin server-side recording:** `record_start_date` and `record_video_active` from `GroupCall` and `updateGroupCall` display a prominent red `REC` badge with tooltip ("Recording … since …") in the call header, call dock, and live bar — immediately on joining an already-recording chat and with a notice cue when recording starts mid-call. Admins with `manage_call` can start recording (audio only, portrait video, or landscape video, with optional title) via `phone.toggleGroupCallRecord` and stop it (noting the file arrives in Saved Messages); tinyclient never records calls locally.
- **Per-person volume, local mute, and per-call noise suppression:** Participant `volume` (`1..=20000`, mapped to `0..=200%`) and `muted_by_you` are keyed by peer (including channels), applied locally through `mediad`, and synced back via debounced `phone.editGroupCallParticipant`. Local **Mute for me** is kept cleanly separate from admin **Mute for everyone** so an admin muting someone locally never mutes them for the room. The Telegram call menu includes a per-call **Noise suppression** toggle defaulting to Settings › Voice.
- **Camera, screen share, multi-picture grid, and video limit:** Telegram group video chats can send a camera (`video_stopped`/`video_paused` via `phone.editGroupCallParticipant`) and screen share (`phone.joinGroupCallPresentation` / `phone.leaveGroupCallPresentation` over a dedicated presentation transport, reusing the existing share picker), and receive multiple remote camera and presentation feeds in the call grid with local pin, fullscreen, and pop-out support (requesting forwarded streams and resolutions over the WebRTC data channel). When `can_start_video` is false or `unmuted_video_count >= unmuted_video_limit`, camera and screen-share controls disable with a clear reason tooltip.
- **Channel live streams (RTMP):** Broadcasts with `rtmp_stream` appear as **Live stream** with viewer count (`participants_count`) in the live bar and join as receive-only viewers (no microphone, cannot speak), fetching segments from `stream_dc_id` via `phone.getGroupCallStreamChannels` and `upload.getFile` (`inputGroupCallStream`) with quality switching (`Low`/`Medium`/`High`) and clear waiting/paused/ended indicators. Admins with `manage_call` can fetch (`phone.getGroupCallStreamRtmpUrl`), copy, reveal, and revoke (`revoke: true`) the server URL and stream key (masked by default and never logged) before starting via `phone.createGroupCall` (`rtmp_stream: true`).

## Signal calls (1:1 and group)

Signal 1:1 voice and video calls and Signal group calls run natively through `mediad` using pure-Rust `str0m` WebRTC (ICE, RustCrypto DTLS-SRTP, Opus 48 kHz audio, and H.264 constrained baseline video) rather than linking Signal's C++ RingRTC fork across five platforms. Avoiding RingRTC keeps tinyclient free of a gigabyte-scale Chromium WebRTC C++ build toolchain on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, and iOS while sharing the same microphone processing (AEC3/NS/AGC2), hardware H.264 / OpenH264 video pipeline, and device pickers as Tiny and Discord calls.

- **1:1 signalling and media:** `OfferMessage`, `AnswerMessage`, `IceUpdateMessage`, `HangupMessage` (Normal, Accepted, Declined, Busy, NeedPermission), `BusyMessage`, and `OpaqueMessage` travel end-to-end encrypted inside Signal's message path. Incoming calls ring natively in the call dock and system notifications with Accept and Decline; accepting on another device (`HangupAccepted`) clears the ring without leaving a missed call; an incoming call while already in another call replies `Busy`.
- **Safety numbers and relay privacy (P7):** Every Signal call shows an end-to-end encrypted lock badge in the call header with the contact's 60-digit Signal safety number (grouped in twelve 5-digit blocks) and verification state. Calls to people not in contacts default to TURN relay-only ICE so your IP address is not exposed; Settings › Privacy › Calls › **Always relay Signal calls** (`signal_always_relay`) forces TURN-only ICE for all Signal calls using credentials from `/v1/accounts/turn`.
- **Group calls:** `DataMessage.groupCallUpdate` and SFU peeks drive the live "in this call · Join" banner in group chats and clear it when the call ends. Joining verifies group membership, checks SFU capacity (surfacing "This group call is full." cleanly), distributes sender keys over 1:1 `CallMessage.opaque` only to verified group members (rejecting rings and keys from non-members), and supports ringing/cancelling members, raising/lowering hands, live emoji reactions, camera toggle, and desktop screen sharing.

## Hand-off

Which call control is native, a hand-off, or missing is [the generated table](../parity.md). A control that table does not mark native names the official app and does not start mediad.

Captions, a soundboard, stage channels, activities, a lobby, and breakout rooms are not built on any network.

## Still open

A native Slack huddle and a native Teams call are not in this tree. Each official path is large, undocumented or unofficial, and none of it is started here. Whether a Slack huddle is worth a spike is still a product choice.

Discord voice from a user account on an unofficial client can lose the account. DAVE makes the client distinguishable, and a picture makes it more so.
