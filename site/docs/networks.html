Networks & terms

What tinyclient does on each network, and what using it may cost you there. Read this before you sign in.

tinyclient is an unofficial client. It is not made, endorsed or supported by Slack, Discord, Telegram, Microsoft or Signal. You use it at your own risk.

  • Discord

    Prohibits third-party clients

    Discord’s terms prohibit third-party clients that use your user account. Discord can lock or ban an account for it, and has. tinyclient only does what you do by hand: it automates nothing, and asks for members and presence only for what is on your screen, as the official app does. That lowers the risk and does not remove it.

    To Discord, tinyclient presents itself as the web client in a browser (on macOS the Safari you have installed, elsewhere a fixed Chrome identity), the same identity the sign-in window used, because one consistent identity draws less attention than an unknown one.

    Use it on an account you can afford to lose access to, or not at all.

    Sign-inDiscord’s web sign-in page, in its own window
    ReadingServers, channels, threads, DMs, friends with presence, avatars, images, files, link cards, reactions, replies, pins, profiles
    WritingText, replies, threads, files, reactions, edit, delete, friend requests, own status, mute, leave
    CallsVoice in DMs and voice channels, with Discord’s end-to-end encryption. Stage channels are not joined.
    Real accountReading, yes. Voice has not yet joined a real call.

  • Slack

    Outside Slack’s API terms

    tinyclient signs in the way Slack’s web app does and uses that session. Slack does not offer this to third-party clients, so it can stop working without notice, and it is outside Slack’s terms for API use.

    If this is a work Slack, your employer’s security policy may not allow unofficial clients. Ask first. Slack’s Enterprise audit logs report unexpected clients and user agents to administrators.

    To Slack, tinyclient presents itself as the web client in the same browser identity the sign-in window used.

    Sign-inSlack’s web sign-in page; every workspace that sign-in holds
    ReadingSections, channels, threads, DMs, avatars, files, reactions, pins
    WritingText, replies, files, reactions, edit, delete, own status, mute
    CallsHuddles open in Slack
    Real accountYes

  • Microsoft Teams

    Your organization decides

    tinyclient signs in as Microsoft’s own Teams client and uses that client’s chat service, which Microsoft does not offer to others.

    Your organization’s policy applies, its administrators can see the sign-in, and an organization can refuse it outright; then nothing tinyclient does helps. Work and school accounts only.

    Sign-inMicrosoft’s sign-in page, in its own window
    ReadingTeams, channels, chats, reply chains as threads, presence
    WritingText, files (through OneDrive), reactions, edit, delete, mute
    CallsCalls and meetings open in Teams
    Real accountNot yet. Built and tested against a local fake written from documentation and other clients’ source.

  • Telegram

    Allows third-party clients

    Telegram permits third-party clients, so this one is within its terms. Those terms also mean tinyclient cannot hide that you read a message or are typing there.

    Telegram asks every client to register an app id. A build carries the id of whoever built it, and Telegram holds that person responsible for what the app does (details).

    Sign-inA QR code scanned with Telegram on your phone, or phone number and code; then your cloud password, if set
    ReadingChats, folders, topics, photos, pins
    WritingText, files, reactions, edit, delete, mute, leave
    CallsOpen in Telegram. Secret chats are not supported.
    Real accountNot yet

  • Signal

    Not offered to third-party clients

    tinyclient links to your account as one more device, the way Signal Desktop does, through presage and Signal’s own libsignal. It never registers a phone number, which would take the account from your phone.

    Signal does not offer its service to third-party clients, and a change on Signal’s servers can stop this working without notice. A linked device receives only what arrives after it was linked: no earlier history. Read receipts and typing are sent only as your Signal settings allow.

    Sign-inLinked as a device: Settings › Linked devices › Link new device on your phone, then scan
    ReadingDirect and group chats, replies, mentions, reactions, edits, deletes. Photos and files appear as a labelled line, not shown.
    WritingText, replies, reactions, edit and delete your own
    CallsNot built
    Real accountNot yet

How tinyclient keeps the risk down

These rules come from the project’s account-safety notes. They lower the risk on networks that do not welcome third-party clients. They do not remove it.

  • One identity per session, the same in the sign-in window, HTTP requests and the socket.
  • Nothing automated. Friend requests, new DMs, joins, reactions and edits happen only when you make them; one click, one request.
  • No enumeration. No whole-server member lists and no history crawl; members and presence only for what is on screen.
  • No polling. Sockets push; requests trace to something you did or to the socket.
  • Respect limits. On a rate limit, wait the full time the network asks. On a captcha, phone or password demand, stop and let you finish in the official app; tinyclient never solves or retries a challenge.

The window shows states (“needs attention”, “signed out”), not warnings. The warnings are here, and in the README.
